# Issue 8: scalar controls and joint-Hessian provenance

Source report: <https://github.com/bbuchsbaum/mixeff/issues/8>.
Engine repair: <https://github.com/bbuchsbaum/mixeff-rs/pull/14>.
Wrapper adoption: <https://github.com/bbuchsbaum/mixeff/pull/6>.

## Reproduction and cause

The fully synthetic 480-observation example in `assessment/issue8-repro.R`
reproduced both reports with engine `004a5d9b12a5d71f483c6c5fc15dbf93ffdb8b7b`
on macOS arm64 / R 4.5.1 (Accelerate). These defects are not confined to the
older R-universe binary named in the issue.

1. The documented scalar `optCtrl$xtol_abs = 1e-8` was rejected when the model
   had two theta parameters. The common engine control validator required an
   exact-length vector. `initial_step` (R's `rhobeg`) had the same defect.
2. A joint-Laplace fit could have status `converged_interior`, report that its
   optimizer Hessian could not be computed, yet provide available fixed-effect
   covariance from `joint_laplace_active_hessian`. The optimizer certificate
   receives no full joint Hessian assessment, while later inference attempts
   its own Hessian. The generic missing-Hessian message incorrectly described
   an intentional omission as a failed computation.

## Repair and meaning of the diagnostics

The wrapper pins published engine commit
`a6b644490a8b83cd871045bb7d3e9065315aefab`, materialized by
`tools/vendor-rust.R`. Singleton absolute tolerances and initial steps are
broadcast after the engine knows the theta dimension. Explicit vectors must
still have that dimension and contain finite positive values. Initial theta
values retain their exact-length requirement. Tests cover LMMs and both GLMM
drivers, heterogeneous vectors, invalid inputs, refits, and serialization.

For joint fits, the optimizer Hessian is now `not_assessed`, with its scope
explained. Overall derivative certification remains `unavailable` when this
assessment is missing. Existing failed or approximate grades remain unchanged;
neither the fit status nor estimator is replaced. Optimization may itself
compute a Hessian for polishing; that does not populate a full Hessian
assessment in this optimizer certificate.

The separate inference stage attempts a central finite-difference Hessian of
the joint minus-twice-log-likelihood over beta and interior theta coordinates.
The fixed-effect covariance is twice the beta block of its inverse. Theta
coordinates too close to their lower bounds for central differences are
excluded from that inference Hessian; this is distinct from one-sided
optimizer KKT evidence. Successful inference does not retroactively certify
the optimizer. R's certificate printout now includes both evidence grades and
their reasons, with wrapped reason text.

Raw gradient norms depend on parameter coordinates. Newton-decrement evidence
is reported separately; neither alone establishes inferential reliability.
The vignette and help pages describe these distinctions and how to inspect
the covariance method, availability, reliability, and notes.

## Remaining scope

This repair does not reproduce the issue's large study model or establish its
runtime, convergence, or scientific adequacy. The compact example is entirely
synthetic. The current `max_feval` budget is shared by initialization and the
joint fit; increasing it may extend initialization. It is not a wall-time cap
or a guarantee for the entire fitting/inference workflow. The R build excludes
NLopt and PRIMA. `start$theta` is not a full beta/theta/mode warm start and does
not bypass initialization. These limitations are now documented.

Separate initializer budgets, richer progress reporting, full warm starts and
a backend-capabilities query remain design requests, not implemented features.
Issue 8 should therefore not be closed as wholly resolved by this patch.
Prior MKL and release-artifact receipts retain their original source scope;
they do not qualify the changed engine bytes for CRAN submission. PR review,
final release-artifact checks, external builder results and merged-head
R-universe qualification remain separate gates.
